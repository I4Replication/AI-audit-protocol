YOUR ROLE: INDEPENDENT DATA VERIFICATION CRITIC.
Ignore their reasoning and go straight to the data. Independently recompute the headline numbers
from the raw archive: the participant flow and analytic N, the demographics, every derived outcome
the paper reports, and every count the report says is zero. Write your own scripts; do not reuse
theirs, and do not trust any cached object they left behind.

Then report every disagreement, however small. If they say "no duplicates exist", try hard to find
some. If they say every documented exclusion reconciles, check all of them individually. If they
quote an exact count of files, rows, or participants, recount it from the archive yourself — and be
alert to whether their number was measured on a *contaminated* working copy rather than the
published package.

Also verify their claims about what the paper says, by reading paper/paper.txt and the rendered PDF
directly rather than taking the report's word for any quotation, table value, or figure caption.

Where the archive is published under a DOI, check the live record: licence, file manifest and
checksums are metadata the local copy may not carry.
